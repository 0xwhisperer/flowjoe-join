Joe is capable of complex research, text reviews, and creative drafting. Here is how you use his core capabilities.

<Note>This page is about chatting with Joe (the robot). To talk with the people in a shared Flow, use [Chat](/workspaces/chat) (the speech bubbles).</Note>

Each send includes more than the prompt you typed. To see the cost and to shrink the tree outline Joe is given, use the token bar and the **Limit map to my location** / **Omit entire flow map** checkboxes above the message box. See [Token usage with Joe](/joe/token-usage).

Chat, Spelling, Transcribe and Images share the target controls below their tabs. Use the crosshair for Flow Chat, or right-click a node inside Joe's target tree to choose Flow, Node or Slot. Choosing a slot in a tool keeps that tab and carries the choice into Chat. See [Choosing Joe's target](/joe/opening-joe#choosing-joes-target) for the slot list, Last used badge and draft behavior.

## Add a requirement while Joe is replying

In your own private OpenAI API chat, keep typing in the prompt while Joe works. Press **Enter** to send a text update for that reply; **Shift+Enter** adds a new line. In docked Joe you can also use the **Send update** arrow beside Stop. For example: “Only change the selected branch” or “Keep the original names.”

The update shows **Queued for this reply**, then **Sending update…**, then **Added to this reply** once Joe has answered with it included. Joe finishes an already-started step before taking the new direction. An earlier proposal that has not started applying is replaced; completed changes are kept. Only one update can wait at a time. Each follow-up uses your selected brain and can incur normal provider charges.

If delivery fails or is uncertain, **Restore update to prompt** puts the text back beside anything you have typed since. Check the conversation before resending an unconfirmed update; FlowJoe does not resend it automatically. **Stop** remains available.

If the update hint is unavailable, keep your draft until Joe finishes or use Stop. This interaction is currently available for private OpenAI API chat, not CLI brains, other providers, shared conversations or guest chat. Changing the brain or API key during preparation makes updates unavailable for that reply.

## Regenerate a reply

Use **Regenerate** on Joe's latest reply to ask again with that earlier message and its saved references. The text and available attachment or context choices you are preparing for your next message stay in the prompt area, including choices changed while Joe replies. Regenerate does not add those unsent choices to the earlier request. References already included in the conversation keep their usual sent status. Unavailable references or context cannot remain selected. It uses your current selected brain and can incur normal provider charges.

## Ask Joe about earlier work

In owner chat and authorized external CLI/MCP sessions, Read or Propose permission lets Joe look up permitted history in the current Flow: earlier conversations, generated-image prompts and model details, retained transcripts, and dates/revisions of verified public changes. Try “Find the chat where we chose the launch name” or “Which image prompt did I use yesterday?” He reads longer results in portions and gives their source dates and conversation/message references. The current chat stays open.

Locked Private sources and Hidden from Joe sources are excluded, as are deleted or uncertain owners and unavailable older copies. After you unlock a Private node, Joe can use it through the normal context, attachment and approved-action controls. Use Hidden from Joe separately if you want to work on the node without giving Joe access. Computer-only history stays local to its owner. Results can be partial, so an empty search does not prove something never existed. Image lookup returns prompt/details without fetching the picture, and requires every recorded source and reference picture to remain available to Joe; version lookup does not restore anything. Authorized external CLI/MCP connections receive these history readers with Read or Propose access. Guests and scheduled runs do not receive them. See [the action reference](/cli/action-reference).

## Document & Asset Analysis

Joe can read files attached to your gallery slots:
- **PDF & Office Analysis:** Ask Joe to summarize text, translate pages, or look up terms.
- **Spreadsheets:** Joe evaluates sheet rows and columns to compile budgets or highlight averages.
- **Images:** Joe inspects images (JPG, PNG) to describe layouts, extract text, or check designs.

Large documents, spreadsheets and calendars are read in sections. Name the heading, sheet and cell range, or dates you need. When Joe reads a PDF from your gallery, he knows which page each passage is on, so you can ask about a specific page ("what does page 12 say?") and he can point you to the page where he found something. For a full review, ask Joe to continue through the remaining available sections and say what, if anything, was left unread.

A text summary covers the text FlowJoe could extract; it does not establish that every page, diagram or embedded image was examined. File-conversion limits are separate from reading the available text. Joe is also told when a PDF page has no readable text, such as a page that is only a chart, photo or scan. In chat he can then look at that page as a picture, so "what does the chart on page 7 show?" gets a real answer. The first time he wants to look at pages of a PDF, Joe asks you, because each page sent as a picture uses noticeably more of your AI usage than reading text; he looks at a few pages at a time, only the ones he needs. If you say no, he answers from the text and tells you the visuals were not read. In a browser, PDF text extraction reads at most the first 100 pages, and Joe is told which pages were left out. For visual details, give Joe the relevant image or screenshot as well.


### Compare saved repository text

With your own OpenAI API key and **Luna 6**, Joe can combine several permitted saved repository text files into a compact comparison. Name the files and the facts you want compared. Joe uses source paths and supporting excerpts to ground the comparison and identifies any file read only in part.

This can reduce AI token usage for larger comparisons, although it may take longer than ordinary reads. Each file read covers at most 12,000 characters, and one reply can coordinate up to eight distinct saved files. For a complete review of a longer file, ask Joe to continue through its remaining sections using ordinary reads. Existing Flow permissions still apply; the admitted text is processed by OpenAI. Single-file questions, open drafts and historical versions use ordinary reads.

## Image Generation

Joe’s **Images** tab gives you a small creative studio beside your references: describe a picture, use an existing image as a guide, and keep useful results together as a stack. It needs an **OpenAI API key**, regardless of your chat brain. Shared guests use the owner’s enabled image connection.

Choose an image model, quality, size, and output format before generating. Background choices depend on the model and format. Use the reference-image control to select an accessible image and enable it for the request.

In the **Images** tab, the compact **Auto**, **New** and **Edit** pills choose how the next request uses the preview:

- **Auto** continues the saved image thread when one exists, or starts a new generation.
- **New** clears the saved preview and thread and starts fresh. It keeps starting fresh until you choose another mode.
- **Edit** edits the image in the preview when a saved thread exists; otherwise it starts a fresh request. Edit can be unavailable when the selected model cannot continue a saved image response.

You can generate graphics directly in your workspace:
- **Prompting:** Ask Joe *"Generate a logo for my coffee shop in sepia style."*
- **Reference Images:** Attach an existing gallery image as a reference to steer Joe's generation style or layout.
- **Write-back Options:** Generated images render inside a chat card. You can click **Send to Gallery** to save it as a new slot, insert it into a stack, or replace an existing image.
- **If generation fails:** Your previous generated picture and its prompt remain available. A failed replacement request does not clear that draft. The new request stays in the prompt box so you can retry it.
- **Stop:** If you stop while the image response is arriving, that late response is not accepted as the finished replacement.
- **Images request timeout:** In the **Images** tab, an image request that has not finished after three minutes stops with a timeout message. Your previous picture stays available, and you can try again. Pressing **Stop** still shows the normal stopped message.
- **Survives a restart:** A generated image you haven't sent to the gallery yet still shows, and can still be sent or reused, after you close and reopen FlowJoe. If a generated image genuinely can't be recovered, the card says so with a plain note instead of showing a blank frame.

<Note>
Encrypted Private-node storage is still under development and is not enabled in the current release. Conversations saved with the node, their archived sessions and compacted memory are being connected to the node’s same encrypted saved content. Older conversations and remaining history/import paths are still unfinished. In the planned encrypted mode, a Private node keeps its unfinished generated picture, prompts and image history under the same node password. Existing saved picture prompts should remain usable when you make the node Private or remove its protection. Removing protection is also intended to keep the generated draft and image history usable normally, without replacing another node’s unfinished draft. Relocking hides those working pictures and prompts; unlocking restores the saved draft. Other older saved copies and remaining protection work must be completed before this mode is released.
</Note>

### Continue an older picture

Open image-generation history from **Images**. Change **Current node** to **All** if necessary, find a picture, and open it to restore its preview, prompt, and saved generation choices. Choose **Auto** or **Edit** to continue where supported, or **New** for a fresh picture. You can send a result to the gallery as a new slot, add it to a stack, or replace an item.

The restored picture, its prompt and its saved details stay together when you reopen Joe. If the picture itself is missing, Joe restores its available prompt history and keeps the missing-picture warning. Pictures made in a chat keep that chat generation’s own prompt history; an unrelated draft in Images is kept separate.

This drawer can find older pictures kept on this computer even when their original node is no longer in the current Flow. **History → Images** is narrower: it lists pictures whose source node is still in this Flow. Guest history always stays within the guest’s shared access.

### Shared image history

Images made through a shared Joe conversation or the **Images** tab are kept with the owner’s Flow, including their image bytes and history entry. The existing image-history drawer shows results whose source is shared with you; reopening or reloading the guest app reads that owner-kept history.

View access can inspect shared history and use the Joe conversation features the owner enabled. Changing shared gallery content and saving a history result into the gallery require Edit access. Saving someone else’s shared result does not change who created it. Removing an image-history entry does not remove a picture still used in shared chat or the gallery; that picture continues to follow its source’s privacy and sharing permissions.

Private sources and hidden stack items are excluded. A public stack item stays available even when a sibling is private. If the source becomes private or access ends while a new history entry is being saved, that entry is refused. Older entries whose source cannot be established reliably are withheld rather than attached to a different item.

## A node's look, and changes in the gallery

You can ask Joe to change the workspace itself. Each of these still waits on an approval card:

- Give a node one of the Theme-menu looks by its menu name. He is shown the real list. A made-up name is refused. Clearing the look lets the node inherit again.
- Copy a slot, or one item out of a stack, and leave the original where it is.
- Crop a picture in its slot. The picture stays in that slot, so any lines on the canvas board stay with it.
- Turn a picture or a note into a PDF and place that PDF beside the original.
- Set, replace, or remove the album art on an audio item, using a picture already saved in FlowJoe. On a linked audio file the cover stays on this machine and does not change the file.
- Color a slot on the canvas board red, green, blue, violet, pink, or cyan, or clear that color.
- Move a canvas card, change its width, or restore its usual width.
- Draw or remove a line between two slots on the same board.

You can ask Joe to control the header player right away, with no approval card. For example:

- “Play some music” starts the current queue, or the first accessible saved playlist if the queue is empty.
- “Shuffle the queue” or “loop this track” changes playback mode. Sequential mode plays in order and repeats the queue; repeat-one loops the current track.
- “Play the audio in the *Opening* node once” plays one chosen gallery audio file and stops when it ends. If there is more than one file in the slot or stack, tell Joe which one.
- “Play something tagged *calm*” works when audio slots have a matching user tag. Joe can use visible tag names and descriptions to match a vibe; he does not infer moods from filenames or node names.
- “Save this queue as *Focus Mix*” creates a named playlist. To save audio carrying a tag without starting playback or changing the current queue, ask “Make a playlist called *Quiet Work* from audio tagged *calm*.” Joe will not overwrite an existing playlist.

Joe can load a saved playlist by its exact name, pause playback, show the player, or hide it (which stops playback). A locked private or hidden-from-Joe node is not available to him.

## Talk with Joe

In a private desktop conversation, open the arrow beside the microphone and choose **Talk with Joe…**, then **Start voice**. An OpenAI API key and a supported Joe voice from **Settings → AI → Joe voice** are required. The microphone starts only after you choose Start voice.

Choose a voice in **Settings → AI → Joe voice**. Additional Live voices show their language and, where applicable, regional accent. Choosing one or pressing its speaker button plays a short billed sample without using your microphone; press again to stop. Live-only voices work in Talk with Joe; Mini and Full choices also support ordinary read-aloud. A changed choice applies to the next voice conversation.

Speak naturally. Your selected chat brain still handles requests and tools, with the conversation’s usual permissions and approval cards. Check the chat and review proposed changes before applying them. Spoken replies alone do not confirm that a change was applied.

- **Mute microphone** stops Joe from hearing you while the conversation stays open.
- **Quiet Joe** silences his voice; **Let Joe speak** turns it back on. This is separate from muting your microphone.
- **End voice** finishes the voice conversation. If Joe is still working, choose whether that work continues in chat or stops.
- Hiding or minimizing Joe, or leaving the Chat tab, ends voice. Work Joe has already started continues in that conversation.
- Choosing **New Chat**, restoring a different conversation, or choosing another node or slot ends voice and stops its unfinished work. Joe saves the spoken transcript before switching. If saving fails, the original conversation stays open for recovery.
- Replacing the Flow or switching between Local and Workspace storage also ends voice. Joe saves before the switch and waits until it finishes before another voice conversation can start.

Live voice costs **$0.05 per minute**, plus your selected brain and tool usage. The voice display shows an estimate; missing final usage is marked unconfirmed. Live voice has no preset conversation-duration or silence limit. It is separate from the **Mini** and **Full** prices for ordinary read-aloud.

Your spoken transcript stays in the conversation. If it cannot be saved, keep the conversation open and use **Try again**. Starting another voice session waits until the save succeeds. If microphone access is denied, allow it in your system settings and try again. If the voice connection is lost, work Joe has already started continues in chat; use the usual **Stop** control to stop it. A lost connection does not undo changes already completed; check the chat before repeating a request.

For speech you want to edit before sending, choose **Dictate message** instead. The microphone’s ordinary dictation behavior and spoken punctuation commands remain available.

When voice dictation is active, you can use spoken commands to structure text:

| Say | Output |
| :--- | :--- |
| *"comma"* | `,` |
| *"period"* | `.` |
| *"new line"* / *"line break"* | Inserts a line break |

## Keep chatting while Joe researches

Keep your conversation moving while Joe researches in the background. Joe stays your collaborator and brings the findings back to you. You can keep talking, change direction, or cancel the research without managing another agent yourself.

With any brain (an OpenAI, Gemini, xAI or Anthropic API brain, or a terminal brain such as Claude Code, Codex, Grok or Antigravity), Joe can ask a read-only Researcher to examine selected gallery references when that helps your task. There is nothing extra to enable or approve: Joe can delegate focused comparisons across several references he has not read yet and brings the findings into his final answer. Routine questions, single-reference lookups, and answers already supported by your conversation stay with Joe.

A compact Researcher card shows the task and where it belongs. Select **Show assignment** while it runs, or **Show findings & references** after completion, to inspect the details and available usage. You can keep chatting with Joe while research runs. **Stop research** appears only while that research is working and cancels that assignment; once findings are in there is nothing left to stop there, and stopping Joe’s reply keeps them. Stopping a different Joe reply leaves research running. If you stop Joe’s reply while he is presenting completed research, the findings stay available in the card. Changed references or access invalidate findings rather than keeping them as current evidence. When findings arrive during a reply, Joe finishes that reply before reviewing and presenting them. You can ask Joe for progress or to cancel and replace the assignment. The researcher cannot edit your Flow, run commands or access references Joe cannot read. Normal approvals still apply to changes Joe later proposes.

The card’s details explain whether research was cancelled, timed out, lost access to its references, encountered an AI service error, returned a result that did not pass FlowJoe’s checks (so it was not used), or stopped because of an unexpected problem. An unavailable reference can prevent research from starting. Ask Joe to try again when you want a new attempt; failed or stopped replies do not restart themselves.

Researchers work over selected gallery references in your own chat (or a guest’s shared chat) on an OpenAI, Gemini, xAI or Anthropic API brain, or a terminal brain, using that same brain and model. On a terminal brain the background work is more runs of the same terminal assistant on your existing login (no API key is needed or used), so it counts against that login’s usage, and the usage shown is approximate. Background work needs Flow access to be on (Read-Only or Propose), and on Antigravity it works on macOS. Terminal brains read pictures for background work where that assistant can accept them: Claude Code, Codex, Grok and Antigravity on a Mac. Antigravity reads those pictures and PDF-page images from the same private temporary image folder used for ordinary chat; FlowJoe deletes it after the run finishes or stops. Guests can use background work too when Joe is on for their invitation and the owner’s brain is an API brain or a terminal brain other than Antigravity: it stays within what the invitation lets Joe read, runs on the owner’s Joe brain and account (on a terminal brain, the owner’s own login), and shows no usage or cost to the guest. A guest on Antigravity keeps Joe’s existing behavior. If the owner changes the selected terminal brain or model before a background job or its combined answer starts, that work stops rather than sending its references or findings to the new brain. Research and Joe’s review are included in the review reply’s receipt (a guest sees no usage or cost, because the owner pays); delegation does not guarantee lower token use or cost.


## Get another perspective with the Reviewer

Joe can ask an independent Reviewer to examine a candidate idea, plan, proposed change, or supplied code. He chooses an approach suited to your question, and you can ask for one explicitly:

| Approach | What it helps with |
| --- | --- |
| **Critique** | Weak assumptions, unclear reasoning, feasibility, and tradeoffs. |
| **Adversarial check** | Counterexamples and conditions under which a claim or plan fails. |
| **Security review** | Specific risks visible in the code and context you provide. |
| **Requirements check** | Whether the proposal meets each stated condition, with missing evidence kept unverified. |
| **Explore alternatives** | Other approaches, their advantages, tradeoffs, assumptions, and open questions. |

For example: “Have a Reviewer challenge this plan,” “Check the security of this supplied handler,” or “Explore other ways we could approach this.” Joe provides the candidate and criteria. The Reviewer receives a focused assignment and supplied material in a separate context; Joe considers the results and brings you his own answer. Routine questions can stay with Joe.

You can use shared gallery references or a passage already in the conversation. The **Reviewer** card shows the assignment. Select **Show assignment**, **Show findings & references**, or **Show alternatives & references** to inspect the details. There is no approach menu or additional permission to manage. Keep talking to Joe while it runs, ask for progress, or use **Stop review** to cancel it. If you stop Joe’s returning explanation, completed results remain available while their material and access are still valid.

A clean review means no supported issues were found within the supplied material. A security review looks at the code and context provided; it does not run the program or inspect unshared files. Exploration returns options rather than treating them as defects, and Joe should leave a choice open when important constraints are unknown. Reviews and suggested fixes do not apply changes or approve publication.

The Reviewer works in your own chat (or a guest’s shared chat) on an OpenAI, Gemini, xAI or Anthropic API brain, or a terminal brain (Claude Code, Codex, Grok, or Antigravity on macOS; for a guest, any of these except Antigravity), with Flow read access, using that same brain and model; on a terminal brain it runs on the assistant’s existing login and its usage is approximate. Guests can use it within their access, on the owner’s account, on the owner’s brain, including a terminal brain other than Antigravity. Reviewer work and Joe’s synthesis are included in the returning reply’s receipt, and the card shows available Reviewer token counts; a guest sees no usage or cost, because the owner pays. An extra perspective does not guarantee correctness, lower token use, or lower cost.

## Background work: several jobs at once

For a request with independent parts, Joe can run up to **three** read-only jobs at the same time, in any mix of Researchers and Reviewers. They appear together as one **Background work** group in the chat, where the work started. The group header shows live counts (for example “2 working · 1 complete”) and **Stop all** when two or more jobs exist and one is still working. Each job is a compact row with its task, Researcher or Reviewer, its status, and **Stop research** / **Stop review** only while that job is working. Use **Show assignment** or **Show findings & references** on a row to open its details, and **Show combined usage** at the bottom for the recorded usage of Joe and the specialist work, including available usage from failed, stopped or replaced jobs. Missing final counts remain marked pending.

While any job is unfinished, or its results are waiting for Joe’s answer, a one-line progress strip sits just above the message box: “Background work · 2 working · 1 complete”, with **Show** and **Stop all** (**Stop** when there is only one job). Click **Show**, or anywhere on the strip, to scroll to the group and move focus to it. **Stop all** stops only jobs that are still working and keeps completed findings. If results arrive while Joe is busy with another reply, the strip says “Joe answers after his current reply”. It leaves once Joe’s combined answer is delivered or there is nothing left to deliver. You can keep chatting the whole time.

When every job has finished, Joe gives **one combined answer**. If a job was stopped or could not finish, he says what was not checked and uses only the completed evidence; nothing is retried automatically. New related work you ask for while the group is open joins it and counts toward the three. Once Joe starts the combined answer, the group is closed and later work starts a new group. If you ask for more jobs than there is room for, Joe starts as many as fit, up to three at once, and says plainly which were not started. If you ask for a fourth job while three are running or waiting, Joe tells you plainly that three is the most at once, that nothing was queued, and offers to wait or to stop one. If you change one request, Joe cancels and replaces only that job. Stopping Joe’s combined reply keeps all completed findings. Jobs that were still working when you closed or reloaded the app show as interrupted and do not resume. Background jobs finish on the brain and model that started them; if you switch brains or models while some are still working or waiting, Joe offers to wait for them or stop them before starting new ones.
