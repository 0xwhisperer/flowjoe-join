---

Just like working with human team members, collaborating with Joe yields the highest quality results when tasks are clearly scoped, context is provided, and completion criteria are explicit. 

Here are the best practices for prompt engineering and workflow automation in FlowJoe.

---

## 🎯 1. Define Explicit Completion Criteria

Joe operates best when prompts have a concrete, checkable definition of success.
- **Vague Prompt:** *"Help me organize my notes."* (This leaves the structure up to the model and can lead to unexpected changes).
- **Effective Prompt:** *"Review my 'Research Notes' node. Create three child nodes under it named 'Timeline', 'Budget', and 'Logistics'. Write a two-sentence summary inside the note card of each new node based on the PDF attached to slot 2."*

By giving Joe precise, executable instructions, you guarantee high success rates.

---

## 🧩 2. Break Down Complex Workflows

For massive refactors or complex outliners:
- **Scope Down:** Do not ask Joe to restructure a 100-node tree in a single prompt. Instead, break it down. Ask Joe to analyze a single branch, review the proposal, approve it, and then proceed to the next branch.
- **Interactive Checkpoints:** Use Joe's chat modal to draft outlines before committing them to the tree. Once you are happy with the draft outline in chat, ask Joe: *"Write this draft outline to the tree under the 'Drafts' root node."*

---

## 🔎 3. Make Tasks Easy to Verify

Ensure your prompts build structures that you can easily audit:
- Ask Joe to create status nodes (e.g. setting node tags like `#ToReview` or `#Draft`) so you can visually isolate the branches he edited.
- For automation scripts and timers, ask Joe to print a log summary in the "Safety Seatbelt" review inbox describing exactly what files or nodes were processed.

---

## 📦 4. Provide Rich Context & Reference Material

FlowJoe's superpower is the **References (Right Brain) Gallery**. Give Joe access to the files he needs:
- **Gallery Slots:** Explicitly direct Joe to files inside your nodes (e.g., *"Read the spreadsheet on slot 3"* or *"Compare the image stack in slot 1"*).
- **Stay on the branch you mean:** Select that node before you send. **Limit map to my location** (on by default) then gives Joe this branch instead of the whole tree, which keeps the send smaller. Uncheck it only when the question needs the rest of the outline. Check **Omit entire flow map** for turns that do not need the tree at all. See [Token usage with Joe](/joe/token-usage).
- **Avoid Ambiguity:** Reference nodes by their exact names so Joe can match them on the map he was given.

---

## 🔒 5. Isolate Sensitive Data with Private Nodes

Before triggering bulk automations or asking broad questions:
- Mark branches containing proprietary code, private credentials, or personal details as **Private** in the tree context menu.
- Private contents stay out of Joe’s Flow access even while you unlock them for yourself. Node names and positions may still appear in the outline, so keep sensitive details out of names. Content you paste into a conversation yourself is still sent.
