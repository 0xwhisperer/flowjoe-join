FlowJoe is easier once you stop treating it like a folder app or a linear note app. Three ideas do almost all the work.

## Structure

The **tree** is structure: roots, child nodes, multi-select, status, themes, frozen nodes, and private nodes. Structure answers *where does this belong?* and *how does this branch relate to that one?*

A node is not just a label. It is a container that can hold meaning and references.

## References

The **gallery** on the selected node is references: images, audio, video, documents, URLs, embeds, stacks, tags, and editable workbenches (spreadsheets, rich documents, calendars).

References answer *what am I looking at, listening to, or citing while I work here?*

## Meaning

**Meaning** sits above raw material:

- Per-node **notes** and **summaries**
- Per-slot notes
- **Tags** that cut across the flow
- The context you deliberately share with **Joe**

Meaning answers *what does this mean for the project?* — not only *what files live here?*

## Why keep them separate

Many tools collapse everything into one list or one canvas. FlowJoe keeps structure and visual reference strong on purpose, then bridges them with selection (pick a node → see its gallery) and with Joe (who can use both when you allow it).
