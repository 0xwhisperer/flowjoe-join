## Work offline

Your local outlines, notes, and saved files remain available without an internet connection. Free local audio transcription also works offline after its engine and model are installed.

Websites, online AI, remote sharing, and model downloads need a connection. Recurring Joe tasks may contact an AI provider while the owning Flow is open and scheduled runs are enabled.

## Choose how to store a Flow

- **Local storage:** FlowJoe manages the Flow and its media in this computer’s app storage.
- **Workspace Folder:** Choose a folder containing the Flow and shared media. You can back it up, sync it with your own service, and use it with external tools. Private content stays on its original computer.

See [Workspace folders](/workspaces/folders) for setup, changing computers, and what happens when another tool changes the folder.

## Keep a portable copy

Export a **.fj** package to take the Flow and its included media together, or choose JSON for a data-only copy. Right-click a node to export just that branch or copy its JSON.

Linked files need separate attention because their paths may not exist on another computer. See [Export & import](/portability/export-import).

## Your mobile file explorer

Think of FlowJoe as a **mobile file explorer for your ideas**. A normal folder carries files; a Flow carries the structure that makes those files useful: the outline, reference gallery, notes, connections, and included media. Your research desk, story bible, project archive, or creative studio can travel in your pocket.

Put your **full `.fj` packages** and a copy of the **FlowJoe app or installer for the computers you use** on a thumb drive. You can pick up your Flow on a compatible computer without bringing your own laptop, signing into a hosted workspace, or rebuilding your collection of references. The same `.fj` package can move between supported desktop platforms; use the appropriate FlowJoe build for each operating system and processor.

### Take a Flow with you

1. Choose **File → Export Flow → Full package (.fj)** and save the package to your thumb drive. Select the history you want to include; you can also protect the package with a password.
2. On the other computer, launch or install its compatible FlowJoe app, then use **File → Import Flow** to open your package. You need permission to run apps on that computer.
3. Explore, organize, and edit your Flow. Included files and local notes are available offline; websites and online AI still need a connection.
4. Before you leave, **export the updated full `.fj` package back to the drive**. Keep an earlier copy if you want a recovery point, then close FlowJoe and safely eject the drive.

The package is a portable snapshot: opening it does not turn it into a file that FlowJoe continually saves back to your thumb drive. Local storage belongs to the computer you are using, even when the app itself is on a removable drive. Export your changes before moving on.

For a working folder that receives saves directly, you can instead keep a **Workspace Folder** on the drive and connect it through **Settings → Data**. Keep the drive connected while working and close FlowJoe before ejecting it. Private content in a Workspace stays on its original computer; review what travels before switching machines.

**Bring the files, not just their paths.** Full packages bundle included imported media. Linked local files remain references: copy their originals separately and relink them on the destination computer. A saved website link also needs internet access to load its live content.

See [Export & import](/portability/export-import) for package choices and password protection, or [Workspace folders](/workspaces/folders) for keeping a live Flow on a drive.

## Choose what leaves your computer

Joe uses a provider you configure. Sharing uses the access you grant to guests. API keys use protected desktop storage when available, with a local browser-storage fallback. See [Security & privacy](/concepts/security) for those boundaries.
