---
title: "Virtual Slots"
description: "Show the same slot in more than one node without copying it."
---

A **Virtual Slot** shows a slot that lives somewhere else in the same Flow. There is one real slot, the **master**, and any number of virtual slots that point to it. They all show the same picture or file, title, notes and tags.

This is useful whenever the same thing belongs in several places. In a family tree, a wedding portrait can sit with both people in it. In a project, the same logo or reference board can sit in every branch that uses it.

---

## Telling them apart

- A **virtual slot** has a dashed outline and a small dashed **V** in its top-left corner, just right of the **+** button (on a stack, right of the item count).
- A **master** that is shown elsewhere has a small dashed **M** in the same place.

Hover over the **V** or **M**, or click it, to see where the slot lives:

- On a **V**: the master's node and slot number, plus any other places it is shown.
- On an **M**: every place it is shown.

Every place in the card is a link. Click one to go to that node and slot. Press <kbd>Esc</kbd> to close the card.

If a virtual slot's master is no longer in the Flow, the card says so. You can remove the virtual slot, or keep it until the master comes back. A virtual slot whose master is in a **Private** node never shows the master's content.

## Adding a virtual slot

1. Click **+** on a slot (or the **+** in the canvas toolbar) to open **Add Slot**, and choose **Virtual Slot**.
2. Pick a node on the left. Its slots appear on the right.
3. Pick the slot you want to show here, and choose **Add Virtual Slot**.

The slot you picked becomes the **master**. If you pick a slot that is itself a virtual slot, the new one points at its master. You can pick a slot from the node you're adding to. Private nodes are listed but can't be opened, and a Private node doesn't offer the **Virtual Slot** card. A virtual slot can't be moved or copied into a Private node either, and a master whose virtual slots live in other nodes can't be moved into one: FlowJoe tells you why and leaves everything where it was.

## What you can do with a virtual slot today

- **Move** it within its node, resize it on the canvas, and connect it to other slots. Where it sits belongs to that node.
- **Remove** it from its node with the trash button or **Remove from This Node…**. The master and any other virtual slots are not touched.
- **Go to Master** (right-click the slot) opens the master's node and slot.
- **Make Real Slot** (right-click the slot) turns it into its own real slot: a copy of the master as it is now, with its own copy of the picture or file. After that it no longer changes with the master. Undo turns it back into a virtual slot. If a file can't be copied, nothing changes and FlowJoe tells you, so the copy never shares the master's file. A file that was already missing is skipped.
- **Copy** it (for example with the copy button on the next slot): the copy is another virtual slot of the same master.

Right-clicking a virtual slot shows the usual slot menu with **Go to Master** and **Make Real Slot** on top. Tags, Crop and Replace change its master. A few actions are greyed out for now: building or changing a stack (Convert to Stack, adding, removing or reordering its items), renaming the file, converting to PDF, inserting clipboard media and the wireframe tool.

Right-click a **master** to see every place it is shown. Each one is a link.

A virtual slot can't go into a stack, and other slots can't be merged into it. **Stack everything** leaves virtual slots out and keeps them as they are.

## Editing through a virtual slot

Changing a virtual slot changes its master, and with it every other place the master is shown. You can edit a virtual slot's **title**, **notes** and **tags**, turn **Hide from Joe** on or off, **replace** or **crop** its picture, **compress** it, and step through a stack to choose which item is on top, right where it is. Clicking its picture opens the master's preview window, where you can edit it too. Which item is on top of a stack belongs to the master, so changing it at a virtual slot changes it everywhere the master is shown.

**Undo** works wherever you are: an edit made at a virtual slot can be undone there, or from the master. Pressing <kbd>Cmd</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> inside the master's notes also undoes notes you typed at one of its virtual slots, because they're the same notes.

If the master is in a **frozen** node, its virtual slots can't be changed either: they say so when you hover over the **V**. To change it, unfreeze the master's node.

If one of the master's virtual slots sits in a frozen node, FlowJoe asks before you change the master: **Change Master Slot?** names the frozen node, because the change will show there too. Clicking into the title or notes and back out never asks. It asks when you leave the title or notes after actually changing them (or before a tag change); until you answer, nothing is changed anywhere. Choose **Save Change** to keep your change or **Cancel** to put the master's text back as it was. Once you've said yes, it doesn't ask again for that slot while you stay on the same node.

A virtual slot that sits in a frozen node can't be changed there, just like everything else in that node. Clicking its picture shows where its master is.

Tagging a whole node from the node menu tags the master of each virtual slot in it too, wherever that master lives, the same as tagging the virtual slot itself. A virtual slot whose master can't be changed (frozen, missing or in a Private node) is skipped. If any of those masters also shows in a frozen node, FlowJoe asks once before tagging, and **Cancel** tags nothing. **Undo** puts every slot's tags back, masters included.

## Search

Searching by tag or text lists every place a slot appears: the master and each of its virtual slots, each in its own node. Virtual slots match on their master's tags, title and notes. Tag filters work the same way: a virtual slot counts as carrying its master's tags.

## Removing slots and nodes

Removing a virtual slot (**Remove from This Node**) takes away only that virtual slot. Its master and every other place it is shown stay as they are.

Removing a master that still has virtual slots elsewhere asks what should happen to them, before anything is removed. This happens whether you remove the slot itself, a selection, every slot in a node, or a whole node. Each master gets its own row with two choices:

- **Make a new master:** one of its virtual slots (you pick which; the first is chosen for you) becomes a real slot with the master's picture, title, notes and tags, and the other virtual slots now show that one. Nothing is lost.
- **Remove all its virtual slots:** they go along with the master.

A virtual slot inside a **Private** node is never offered as the new master; if it's the only place left, the row removes it. If one of those virtual slots sits in a **locked** Private node, FlowJoe names the node and asks you to unlock it first, before anything is removed.

If one of those virtual slots sits in a frozen node, the row says so, and confirming changes that node too. **Cancel** removes nothing. **Undo** brings back the master and every virtual slot as they were.

A master that has virtual slots can't be merged into a stack, because its virtual slots would lose it.

## Virtual slots and guests

When you share a node with a guest, its virtual slots show their master's picture, title, notes and tags, just as they do for you.

- If the guest can also see the node the master is in, the hover card looks exactly like yours, with working links.
- If the master's node isn't shared with them (including a part of the Flow you left out of their share), they still see the content, but the hover card says they don't have access to the node the master is on. It never shows that node's name or where it sits in your Flow, and it lists only the places the guest can see.
- A master in a **Private** node, or a hidden master slot, is never shown to them.

A guest can change what a virtual slot shows only when they can edit the node its master is in. They can still use **Make Real Slot** to get their own copy in the node they're working in, with its own copy of the picture or file, and change that copy as they like — unless the master is in a Private node or hidden. If a file can't be copied on your computer, nothing changes and their change is refused. They can still move it, reorder it, connect it or remove it in the node they're working in. A guest can remove a master slot too, with the same choice you get (make one of its virtual slots the new master, or remove them all), as long as every virtual slot of it is in a part of the Flow they can see and change. If any sits outside what you shared with them, or in a frozen node, only you can remove it, and FlowJoe tells them so. When a guest edits a master that has a virtual slot in a frozen node outside their share, they get the frozen-node warning without that node's name.

## Exporting a node with virtual slots

When you export a node on its own (or use Copy Node JSON), a virtual slot whose master lives elsewhere in your Flow brings that master's picture, title, notes and tags along with it, so the export never points at something that isn't there. A master that is hidden, or sits in a hidden or Private node, is never included in the export; that virtual slot travels as a bare pointer.

## Making a node Private

A virtual slot can't point into or out of a Private node. So when you make a node Private and some of its slots are shown in other nodes, FlowJoe asks first, before the password step:

- **A master shown in other nodes:** make one of those virtual slots the new master (it gets its own copy), or turn them all into real slots. Either way, the Private node keeps its own copy. A virtual slot that is itself in a Private node can't become the new master.
- **A virtual slot whose master is outside:** it becomes a real slot, a copy of its master.

Every copy gets its own copy of the picture or file, so nothing is shared between the Private node and the rest of your Flow. The one exception is a rich document's embedded pictures: they stay shared with the master, the same as when you make a real copy. **Cancel** changes nothing, and undoing Make Private puts the slots back as they were. If nothing crosses the edge, Make Private works exactly as before. If one of the slots is in a locked Private node, FlowJoe asks you to unlock that node first.

## Importing a node with virtual slots

When you import that file again — with **File › Import** (adding it to your Flow or replacing it), **Import Node(s)**, or by dropping the `.fj` file on the tree — FlowJoe decides for each virtual slot in it:

- **Its master is in the file too:** it stays a virtual slot of that master.
- **Its master is still in this Flow and hasn't changed:** it quietly reattaches to it as a virtual slot again.
- **Its master is still in this Flow but has changed since you exported:** the **Imported Virtual Slots** window lists each one, with a small picture of the master, where the master is ("Master in Rose Hartley · Slot 1") and what changed (for example "notes changed" or "picture replaced"). For each one choose:
  - **Stay virtual** (the default): shows the master as it is now, and follows it from here on.
  - **Own slot:** keeps the version you exported as a separate real slot.

  **Set all** switches every row at once. **Import N Slots** goes ahead; **Cancel** stops the whole import and nothing is added.
- **Its master isn't in this Flow** (another Flow, or the master was deleted), **or it's now in a Private node, or the slot lands in a Private node:** it comes in as its own real slot, with the picture, title, notes and tags it had when you exported. If the master's node was hidden from Joe, the new slot's notes are hidden from Joe too. If the file has no pictures packed in it and the picture isn't on this computer either, that slot comes in as a virtual slot with its master missing instead, so the import still goes ahead.

A virtual slot can't live inside a Private node, so one that lands in a Private node becomes its own real slot even when its master came in the same file. Several virtual slots of the same missing master stay together: the first becomes the real slot and the others show it. A virtual slot whose master was Private when you exported carried nothing, so it comes in as a virtual slot with its master missing.

## Joe and virtual slots

Joe works with virtual slots the same way on every AI model you pick.

- **Joe reads a virtual slot as a pointer.** He knows which slot it shows and where that master lives, and he reads the master's picture, title and notes once, at the master, rather than once for every place it appears.
- **A master Joe can't see stays hidden.** If the master sits in a node you've hidden from Joe (or a Private node), Joe only knows the slot is a "virtual slot of a slot hidden from Joe". He sees nothing about it, can't read it, and can't add another virtual slot of it.
- **A guest's Joe sees what the guest sees, and never anything hidden from Joe.** In a shared Flow, the guest's Joe reads a virtual slot's master whenever the guest can see it, including a master outside what you shared that the guest is shown, but never one in a node you've hidden from Joe; it never learns where an outside master lives. It can copy such a virtual slot and use **Make Real Slot** on it, just as the guest can, except when its master is hidden from Joe.
- **Ask Joe to show a slot in another node** ("put the wedding portrait in Walter too") and he proposes a new virtual slot of it. Asking him to copy a virtual slot makes another virtual slot of the same master. He can also propose **Make Real Slot** when you want one place to stop changing with the master.
- **Changes go to the master.** When Joe changes what a virtual slot shows (its title, notes, tags or picture), he changes the master, so every place it is shown changes. If the master is also shown in a frozen node, the proposal card says so, and choosing **Apply** is your go-ahead.
- **Joe's button on a virtual slot** opens Joe on its master. Pictures Joe makes, text you send into the slot, and dictation corrections all land on the master. If the master is shown in a frozen node, FlowJoe asks first, just as it does when you edit the master yourself. If the virtual slot can't change its master (the master is frozen, missing or Private), Joe isn't opened on it.
- **Removing a master is your choice.** Joe can propose removing a master slot that has virtual slots, but he never decides what happens to them. The proposal card says you'll choose, and when you choose **Apply**, FlowJoe shows the same **Remove Master Slot** window you get yourself: make one of its virtual slots the new master, or remove them all. **Cancel** leaves everything as it was. Joe can't remove such a master when one of its virtual slots is in a node hidden from him or in a frozen node, and a scheduled task never removes one on its own. A guest's Joe works the same way for the guest: the guest makes the choice in the same window, and a master that is also shown somewhere the guest can't change gets the same "only the owner can remove it" message the guest sees when removing it themselves.

The FlowJoe command line and outside assistants connected over the Agent Bridge get the same actions and the same rules. Removing a master is held until you choose in the app when you approve it. A command-line session you started with `--autoapprove` applies the choice it sent (make a new master, or remove them all) without asking.



What you hide from Joe stays hidden from every Joe, including a guest’s Joe and scheduled tasks. This keeps your guest’s own view and actions available: a guest can still use Make Real Slot and remove a master when their normal edit and removal rights allow it. Joe keeps the node’s name and place, but receives none of its hidden content.
