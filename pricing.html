Tier Reasoning

This mockup demonstrates a hybrid local-first business model inspired by Obsidian and Figma. The single-player application remains completely uncompromised, while real-time multiplayer networking acts as the monetization bridge.

1. Solo Creator ($0)
  • 100% offline and private.
  • No account or login required.
  • Workspace Folders and plugins stay free forever.
  • Preserves user trust and builds organic word-of-mouth.
2. FlowJoe Pro ($8/mo)
  • The natural upgrade point when sharing with clients.
  • Provisions an automated outbound tunnel with no router setup.
  • Includes a custom personal address (e.g. dan.flowjoe.live).
  • Guests join free in their browser without an account.
  • Includes a risk-free 7-day trial with no credit card required.
3. FlowJoe Team ($15/seat)
  • Built for business continuity and studio coordination.
  • Priced at the Figma-standard $15/seat ($30/mo for duos).
  • Enables decentralized desktop-to-desktop Peer Node meshing.
  • Every team seat can host sessions and share links independently.
  • Centralized billing to add or reassign licenses.
  • Pools company AI keys and allows project ownership transfer.
4. Zero-Cloud Economics (90%+ Margins)
  • FlowJoe hosts zero databases or user media storage.
  • All files, videos, and notes stay on the host's hard drive.
  • Cloudflare's network only carries live sessions while they are open, and stores nothing.
  • Near-zero server costs yield massive gross margins on $8/month.
  • Enables sustainable pricing without aggressive price hikes.
5. The Guest Viral Engine
  • Hosts pay for Pro to share boards with clients and reviewers.
  • Guests join free in their browser with zero app installation.
  • Every session exposes creative professionals to the live app.
  • Guests download the free desktop app for their personal work.
  • Paid Pro hosts become FlowJoe's primary organic growth engine.
6. Offline Immunity & Permanent Independence
  • The app never locks down during internet outages or travel.
  • Canceling a subscription never restricts access to your own work.
  • Users can work uninterrupted on flights, remote locations, and secure sets.
  • Removes the dread of platform lock-in and hostage data.
  • Builds lifelong user trust that cloud SaaS competitors cannot match.
💡 Key Question 1: Are Pro Guests View-Only, or Can They Edit for Free?
  • Pro guests should edit for free, capped at 3 live editors per session.
  • Charging per guest creates high friction for simple client reviews.
  • Three editors covers the classic creative trio: Host (or Co-Host), Client, and Lead Reviewer.
  • If a host designates a Co-Host, that Co-Host occupies 1 of the 3 active editor seats (leaving 2 for clients). All other joiners participate as viewers.
  • The 3-editor cap prevents entire studios from sharing a single host license without upgrading to Team.
🔒 Key Question 2: Why Choose a 7-Day Trial Over 14 Days, and How Do We Stop Burner Accounts?
  • Why not 14 days: A two-week trial lets users finish an entire client project for free and abandon the app without paying.
  • Why 7 days works better: One week covers a standard Monday-to-Friday work sprint, creating prompt decision urgency.
  • Salted, privacy-preserving machine token: To allow completely credit-card-free trials without serial burner abuse, the app hashes the stable OS installation ID with an irreversible salt (never reading volatile swappable parts like RAM or drives). No raw serials are transmitted or stored, protecting user privacy while enforcing 1 trial per machine per year.
  • Subdomain friction: Cycling burner accounts breaks the host's permanent personal address (dan.flowjoe.live), creating natural friction against abuse with live clients.
  • No hostage data: When a trial expires, all local flows and notes stay completely accessible, pausing only the live web sharing.
🛡️ Key Question 3: Why Do Studios and Agencies Choose This Over Cloud SaaS?
  • Zero cloud leakage: Confidential scripts, game lore, and pitch decks never reside on central company servers.
  • Private AI compliance: Studios bring their own private API keys or local models, guaranteeing client IP is never used for training.
  • Permanent asset control: All projects save as standard files on local drives, ensuring company work survives even when team members move on.
  • Ephemeral live streams: Invited guests view encrypted real-time streams without storing source files in external cloud folders.
🤝 Key Question 4: How Does This Solve Client Review Friction?
  • Zero account barrier: Clients click a single browser link and review the board immediately without creating a login.
  • No software installation: External stakeholders and directors never need to install desktop software for a quick review.
  • Presenter follow mode: Clients never get lost on massive canvas layouts because their view tracks the host automatically.
  • Real-time feedback: Clients type directly into shared notes and arrange cards live, replacing slow rounds of marked-up PDFs.
🏢 Key Question 5: What Is the Distinction Between Team ($15/seat) and Studio (20+ Seats)?
  • What a Team gets ($15/seat, $30/mo min): Fluid daily coordination for 2–20 creators, independent host addresses, decentralized peer-to-peer tree meshing, pooled host AI keys, and branch ownership handoff.
  • What a Studio gets (20+ Seats): Volume discounts ($10–$12/seat in bulk), annual invoicing/ACH, SAML SSO, self-hosted private in-network packet relay containers, and zero-telemetry local LLM compliance.
  • Architectural consistency: Both tiers use the exact same local-first peer protocol. A Studio relay routes encrypted packets inside the company's network, never storing project files or databases.
🎟️ Key Question 6: Why the $15 Project Pass is an Essential Part of the Product
  • The creator pattern: Indie game developers and fiction writers work alone for months, needing collaboration only during client review or pitching sprints.
  • Overcoming subscription resistance: Solo makers who resent recurring monthly commitments will gladly pay a one-time $15 pass for 30 days of active sharing.
  • Zero purchase limit & stacking: Users can buy as many Project Passes as they wish throughout the year. If a user buys another pass before the current one expires, the remaining days stack seamlessly (+30 days). The math works heavily in FlowJoe's favor ($15 for 30 days = $0.50/day vs $0.26/day on Pro Annual).
  • Natural upgrade gravity: Once a creator buys 3 or 4 passes in a year ($45–$60), they naturally realize that upgrading to Pro Annual ($72 founder or $96 regular) keeps their tunnel open year-round for less friction.
  • Zero commitment lock-in: The pass expires automatically without renewal traps, leaving all local files untouched on the host's drive.

🤝 Key Question 8: How Does FlowJoe Handle Creative Duos (No 5-Seat Minimum Cliffs)?
  • The SaaS trap we avoid: Most collaborative enterprise tools impose an artificial 5-seat minimum, forcing a 2-person creative partnership or co-founding duo to pay $75–$100/month. This breeds resentment and forces users to share single logins.
  • The stepping stone ladder: A creator starts on Solo ($0), moves to Pro ($8/mo) for solo client reviews, adds a Co-Host (+ $6/mo) for partner presenting, and steps up to Team ($30/mo for 2 seats) when they build a full peer-to-peer studio workflow.
  • The $30/month duo math: FlowJoe Team starts at a true 2-seat minimum. A duo pays just $30/month total ($360/year billed annually), giving both creators full independent host tunnels, pooled AI keys, and live peer-to-peer meshing.
  • Decentralized Peer Nodes: In the Team plan, creators can mount each other's live branches directly into their desktop trees as Peer Nodes—enabling live desktop collaboration without requiring central cloud database storage.
⚖️ Key Question 9: Why Don't Teams Just Use Pro with Free Browser Edit Guests?
  • The asymmetry barrier: On Pro ($8/mo), the host is the sole master. A browser guest is an ephemeral visitor on someone else's machine. The guest owns no master files, has no desktop app workspace, and cannot host their own sessions.
  • The native desktop difference: Team members are equal creators who build every day. On Team, they work inside their native desktop app, mount colleagues' branches as live Peer Nodes, work offline with local queued changes (which send automatically on reconnect), and host their own simultaneous reviews.
  • Bulk pricing for large studios (20+ seats): For studios and agencies needing 20+ to 100+ seats, FlowJoe offers tiered bulk volume pricing (e.g. $10–$12/seat), annual invoicing/ACH, custom SAML SSO, and on-premise private relay deployment.
