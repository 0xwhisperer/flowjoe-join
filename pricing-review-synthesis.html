Product Strategy & Pricing Dialogue

FlowJoe Pricing: Comprehensive Synthesis of Shalom's Review

A detailed exploration of user instincts, real codebase capabilities, and launch strategy.

This document provides a thorough, item-by-item exploration of the feedback provided by Shalom in her structured review. It pairs her sharp practical instincts—such as subscription resistance, viral exit prompts, and conversion friction—with the engineering realities of FlowJoe's current codebase and the commercial structure established for launch.

1. Tiers: Launch with Solo + Pro First vs. Showing Team Tier Structure

Shalom's Perspective
"All these features require more overhead and development to build and maintain... At minimum, we could start with the Pro plan, and put Team on a feature roadmap. I don't see a need to launch with it."

She suggests that solo and small businesses might not buy full team tiers, and that corporate administrative features (member management, pooled keys, license transfers) could push a 2–4 person pod away prematurely. Her alternative is a two-tier model: Solo ($0) and Pro ($8 or $5), with an "add an editor seat" fee for anyone needing more than 3 editors.

Deep Dive & Codebase Reality

Her instinct to avoid premature development overhead is sound advice for typical SaaS companies. However, in FlowJoe's specific case, the flagship capability of Team—Peer Nodes—is already ~90% built in our codebase today:

  • Branch Sharing is built: src/electron/collaboration/guestRequestPath.js already implements and verifies subtree-scoped sharing, so an owner can share any branch of their tree while keeping the rest private.
  • Offline Queuing & Replay is built: src/js/guest/guestOpsSync.js and guestLiveSync.js already buffer edits in IndexedDB when disconnected from the host, and automatically replay/flush them when reconnecting via POST /flow/ops.
  • The remaining 10%: Simply surfacing teammate addresses inside the desktop app as a Peer Node type so one user can mount another's live branch directly in their local tree.

Strategic Value of Keeping Team Visible: Team is not just an administrative bundle; it represents true decentralized desktop-to-desktop meshing. Keeping Team on the pricing page ($15/seat/mo, 2-seat minimum = $30/mo) frames the full product ladder. It signals that FlowJoe is built for serious creative teams, while immediately making the $8/mo Pro plan look remarkably affordable by comparison.

2. Price Point Defense ($8 vs. $5 or Launch Promo) Pricing Strategy

Shalom's Perspective
"I agree with the general model, I just don't know if our price point is defensible yet (as a startup). We might start lower, say $5 for pro, or offer an early adopter promo."

She questions whether a brand-new startup can command $8/month from creators and freelancers before proving massive market fit, suggesting either a $5 introductory tier or a special early adopter promo.

Deep Dive & Market Positioning

Why $8/mo is Already Exceptionally Defensible:

  • FlowJoe Pro ($8/mo annual, $96/yr | $10 monthly) provisions a dedicated, zero-config outbound tunnel (name.flowjoe.app) with permanent address routing and live WebSockets.
  • In the creative tool market, $8/month sits well below established benchmarks: Figma is $15/seat, Miro is $8–$10/seat, and Loom is $12.50/user.
  • Dropping the baseline price to $5 anchors the perceived value of the product too low, attracts high-churn hobbyists, and makes later price adjustments difficult without creating meaningful additional conversion volume.

The Winning Move (Her Promo Idea): Rather than lowering the official sticker price to $5, we can use her suggestion of an Early Adopter / Founding Member Promo. For example: "Launch Special: $72 for your first year ($6/mo)" or a locked-in founder discount. This creates launch urgency and drives early cash flow without devaluing the permanent $8/$10 tier.

3. The Zoom / Screen-Share Alternative User Behavior

Shalom's Perspective
"What stops someone from just relying on zoom and screen-sharing, rather than using our paid tunnel? We may need to extra-insentivize someone to go through the process of setting up an account in order to receive the trial."

She raises a very real user-behavior hurdle: if a freelance creator can just hop on a Zoom, Google Meet, or Discord call and share their monitor for free, why would they bother setting up a FlowJoe account and paying for live tunnels?

Deep Dive & Value Proposition

This is one of the sharpest questions in her review because it pinpoints the difference between passive broadcasting and active collaboration:

  • Passive Screen Sharing (The Zoom Trap): A screen share is completely one-way. The client cannot navigate at their own pace, cannot zoom into high-res artwork, cannot click between tabs or spreadsheet cells, and cannot leave organized notes. They are forced to watch the host’s mouse cursor and say "can you scroll down a little?"
  • FlowJoe Live Sharing (The Interactive Workshop): When a client opens a FlowJoe link in their browser, they are inside the live canvas. They can explore the tree independently, arrange cards, drop reference files, and type directly into shared notes. Presenter Follow Mode allows the host to guide them when needed, while still letting the client participate.

Actionable Copy Tweak: Our marketing and onboarding copy must explicitly hit this contrast: "Stop asking clients 'can you see my screen?'. Give them an interactive board where they can explore, arrange cards, and leave feedback directly on your work."

4. Plugin Safety & Governance Architecture & Trust

Shalom's Perspective
"Community plugins could introduce security risks so we should tread carefully, particularly if it requires significant oversight on our end. This feature could be a paid upgrade opportunity or add-on."

She expresses valid caution that community-contributed plugins could open security holes, require costly engineering oversight, or potentially leak user data, suggesting plugins could be gated behind a paid tier.

Deep Dive & Architectural Proof

Her concern is completely natural when thinking about script-heavy extension ecosystems (like Obsidian or VS Code), where plugins can execute arbitrary Node.js scripts or make rogue background HTTP requests.

However, FlowJoe solves this at the structural root: FlowJoe plugins are purely declarative.

  • Zero Outbound Network Access: Plugins have no network access, no fetch APIs, and no ability to open sockets or communicate externally.
  • Declarative Grammars Only: A plugin in FlowJoe defines custom metadata fields, layout templates (e.g. tabletop RPG character sheets or screenplay beats), Joe prompt assistants, and export formatters.
  • Zero Server Oversight Required: Because plugins cannot run arbitrary background code or exfiltrate projects, they cannot leak confidential creative decks, scripts, or client files. Keeping them free in the Solo tier maintains user trust and drives organic community adoption.

5. Trial Abuse & Machine Privacy Anti-Abuse

Shalom's Perspective
"Hardware ID locks are easy to get around (reinstalls, VMs, new machines) and raise privacy questions for a 'zero-telemetry' brand."

She warns that inspecting hardware serials conflicts with FlowJoe’s privacy-first brand promise, while being easily bypassed by technical users spinning up virtual machines or fresh installations.

Deep Dive & The Salted Token Solution

Shalom nailed the exact friction point of raw hardware tracking. We reached the same conclusion in our latest architectural audit and explicitly redesigned the verification flow:

  • No Raw Hardware Serials: The app never reads or transmits hardware serial numbers, MAC addresses, RAM module serials, or disk UUIDs. Swapping out computer parts will never penalize or lock a user out.
  • Salted OS Installation Token: The desktop app creates a local, irreversible hash using the OS installation identity combined with an application salt (crypto.createHmac('sha256', salt).update(osInstallId)). No raw machine identifiers ever leave the computer.
  • Subdomain Friction: Creating burner accounts breaks the user's permanent personal tunnel handle (dan.flowjoe.app). Professional creators presenting to real clients will not constantly change their public address just to save $8/month.

6. The $15 Project Pass (30 vs. 45 Days) Billing Flexibility

Shalom's Perspective
"I like the proposal of an alternate option to a subscription, as a subscription avoider myself. xD If we simplify the payment plan to just a Pro tier with this as an alternative, that could be compelling. I would suggest $15 for 45 days."

She validates the non-recurring pass as a compelling option for subscription-resistant creators, suggesting extending the duration from 30 days to 45 days.

Deep Dive & Economic Balance

It is huge validation to see a self-described "subscription avoider" get excited about the Project Pass. Many independent makers, consultants, and contractors experience feast-or-famine cycles and dread recurring monthly charges.

Why 30 Days is the Ideal Window:

  • The Sprint Horizon: 30 days perfectly encompasses a standard creative gig, client pitch cycle, or festival submission window.
  • Subscription Economics: If $15 gives 45 days, that equals $10/month without any recurring commitment. That would severely undercut the monthly Pro plan ($10/mo) and cannibalize annual subscriptions ($8/mo).
  • At $15 for 30 days, the user pays a fair premium for zero commitment, while FlowJoe earns higher gross revenue per active project day.

7. Viral Growth & Guest Conversion Growth Engine

Shalom's Perspective
"If guests join without the need for an account, where/when do we promote or suggest they download our product? How do we market to them? Do we create an affiliate/rewards/discount program?"

She suggests placing a download prompt on the exit or "session ended" screen after a guest has experienced the product, and exploring Dropbox-style referral rewards (e.g., earn a free month when an invited guest becomes a paid user).

Deep Dive & Viral Loops

This is the single most actionable marketing recommendation in her review:

  • The "Session Ended" Moment: Interrupting a client while they are actively reviewing a board feels tacky and unprofessional. But the moment the host ends the call and the guest closes the board, an elegant exit screen appears: "Presented with FlowJoe. Want an infinite local workspace for your own notes and projects? Download the free desktop app." They have just seen the app work live with zero lag—this is the highest-converting possible moment.
  • Dropbox-Style Host Referral: Because FlowJoe’s zero-cloud architecture means marginal hosting costs are near zero, granting a paid Pro host 1 Free Month when an invited client subscribes is practically cost-free for us. It incentivizes design studios, freelancers, and agencies to mandate FlowJoe with all their clients.

Strategic Synthesis & Key Action Items

  • Implement the Guest Exit Screen: Design a polished, non-intrusive download prompt on the guest "Session Ended" screen.
  • Prepare a Launch Promo: Keep the published sticker price at $8/mo ($96/yr), but offer a limited-time "Early Adopter / Founding Member" annual deal during launch week.
  • Position Active Canvas vs. Passive Zoom: Emphasize that FlowJoe turns one-way screen shares into live, interactive workshops where clients can click, arrange, and brainstorm.
  • Highlight Peer Nodes in Team: Explain that Team provides decentralized peer-to-peer tree meshing (which is ~90% built already), clarifying that it’s not just an administrative seat bundle.
  • Maintain Current Architecture: Our existing decisions around declarative plugins, salted OS verification tokens, and 30-day Project Passes successfully resolve all technical risks Shalom raised.
